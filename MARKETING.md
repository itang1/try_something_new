# Try Something New: Marketing and growth plan

Written 2026-10-31. The tasks that come out of this plan live in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) under the Growth area; this file is the why and the how.

**An honest note first.** No plan can make an app popular. Most consumer apps never break out, including good ones. What a plan can do is put the app in front of the right people at the right moment, make it easy for each happy user to bring in the next one, and measure what's working so effort goes there. Everything below is aimed at those three things.

## 1. What made Lovable take off, and what carries over

Lovable is an AI tool that builds web apps from a chat. It grew very fast in 2024 and 2025; its revenue milestones were widely reported as some of the fastest of any startup. I haven't verified those figures, so this plan doesn't lean on them. The lessons below come from how it grew, which is public, not from the numbers.

| What Lovable did | Why it worked | What it means for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Built an audience before the product.** It started as GPT Engineer, an open-source project that drew a large following on GitHub before there was a company. | Thousands of people already knew the founder and the idea on launch day. | Start collecting people now: a waitlist page, a TestFlight beta, and posting the build. Launch to a list, not to silence. |
| **A one-line promise you can picture.** "Describe an app, get an app." | People repeat a sentence they understand in one hearing. | Ours: "One new thing to try near you, one card at a time." Every screenshot, video and post says this. |
| **The result is the advertisement.** Every app someone built had a live link, and free-plan apps carried a small "Edit with Lovable" badge (as I understand it; check before quoting). Users posted what they made. | Each user's result was a public demo with the brand on it. Growth came from users' posts, not ads. | Our result is *the thing you did*. Turn it into a good-looking card (photo, activity, city, "12 things tried in Seattle") with our name on it, one tap from the share sheet. This is the most important product work in this plan. |
| **Founder-led, build-in-public content.** The founders posted progress, milestones and lessons on X and LinkedIn constantly. | A person is more interesting to follow than a logo, and milestones are news. | You post the series, not a brand account voice: "I let my app pick what I do every weekend for a year." |
| **Fast time to "wow."** You saw something working in about a minute. | The free trial sells itself. | Show the first idea card in under 10 seconds, before asking for location or anything else. |
| **A community that showed off.** Discord, launch weeks, hackathons, a gallery of what people built. | Users became recruiters and teachers. | A monthly "try-along" (everyone tries the same thing), a gallery of shared cards, and local meetups in the launch city. |
| **Perfect timing.** It rode the AI coding wave just as "vibe coding" became a phrase everyone used. | Demand already existed; it caught it. | Our wave is **New Year's resolutions**: "try new things" is one of the most common ones every January. Launch publicly in early January, not in November. |
| **A reason to come back daily.** Free daily credits meant people returned. | Habit without nagging. | We already have the daily idea widget and weekend ideas. Keep the no-streaks rule; the pull is a new idea, not guilt. |

**What doesn't carry over.** Lovable sells a tool to makers, who are online, love to show their work and pay for it. We serve people who want to get *offline*. Our users post less about apps and more about experiences, so the share card has to be about their experience with our name small in the corner, not about the app. And because the app is private and has no accounts, our referral loop has to work without tracking people (section 7).

**Aiming higher than "like Lovable."** The realistic way to beat a typical app's growth is to win one city completely before the next, the way local apps have done for years, and to own one calendar moment (January) every year. That compounds; a single viral spike doesn't.

## 2. Positioning

- **One line:** One new thing to try near you, one card at a time.
- **Longer:** Stuck doing the same things every weekend? Try Something New suggests something you've never done, shows the closest place to do it (or how to do it at home), and keeps a checklist and memories of everything you've tried in your city.
- **What it isn't:** not an events app (nothing expires), not a review site, not a feed.
- **Proof points to repeat:** 102 activities, tens of thousands of real places, mostly free or cheap, no account, no ads, no streaks.

## 3. Who it's for, in order

1. **People who just moved to a city.** They search "things to do in Seattle" and join city subreddits and newcomer groups. Easy to find and highly motivated.
2. **Couples looking for date ideas.** A large, evergreen search ("date ideas near me"), and every plan involves a second person who sees the app.
3. **New Year's and bucket-list people.** "30 before 30", "try 52 new things this year". January peak.
4. **Friend groups stuck in a rut.** Shared plans spread the app inside the group.
5. **Seoul.** A separate market with its own channels (Instagram, Naver blogs, Threads, KakaoTalk) and its own language. Treat it as a second launch, not part of the US one.

## 4. The shareable moment

Build these in this order; each is on the roadmap.

1. **"Tried it" card.** After saving a memory: your photo, the activity's icon and name, the city, your rating and "#12 in Seattle". Story-sized (9:16) and square. App name and a short link in the corner.
2. **City bucket list card.** Your checklist as a poster, done items lit up. People share progress toward a goal.
3. **Monthly recap and Year in review.** A short story-style sequence in December. Year-in-review formats are widely shared every December, and this one is about real life, not screen time.
4. **Send a friend an idea.** "You'd love this: pottery at X" with a link that opens the idea in the app, or a web page with a store link if they don't have it.

## 5. Launch plan

**Private beta (November to December).** TestFlight and Play internal testing, 50 to 200 people, almost all in the launch city. Recruit from friends, a waitlist page and local communities. Goal: learn whether people actually go and do things, and collect real shared cards and quotes for the store page and the launch.

**Public launch (early January).** App Store and Google Play together, with the New Year angle: "Try 52 new things this year."

**Channels, roughly in order of expected value for this app:**

- **Short video (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts).** The founder series: "My app picked my weekend: pottery in Fremont." One idea, the place, the honest result, 20 to 40 seconds. Post three to five a week from beta onward; it takes many posts before any one lands, so consistency matters more than polish.
- **Reddit, carefully.** City subreddits and r/[city] newcomer threads ban self-promotion, so read each sub's rules and lead with something useful, like a free "102 things to try in Seattle" list on the web with the app mentioned once. Ask moderators before posting about the app itself. Communities like r/SideProject and r/reactnative are fine for the build story.
- **Local partners.** Pottery studios, climbing gyms, run clubs, board game cafés and the like. Offer a free "Featured on Try Something New" sticker with a QR code and a page that sends their customers to the app. They gain visitors; we gain installs from people who are already out trying things.
- **Local newsletters, blogs and creators.** City newsletters and small local creators (a few thousand followers) are reachable and trusted. Pitch a story, not an ad: "a local made a free app that turns the city into a checklist."
- **Product Hunt.** Good for credibility, feedback and a launch-day spike from tech-curious people; not where most of our users are. Launch there on a weekday in the launch week, with the founder story.
- **Build in public on X, LinkedIn and Threads.** Milestones, lessons and real numbers (beta testers, places tried). This is how you meet other builders, journalists and potential partners.

**City by city.** Launch in one city, get it to the point where people there hear about the app from more than one place, then move to the next. Each new city gets its own short launch: a city page, partners, a local creator, a subreddit post and a store listing screenshot set for that city.

## 6. App Store optimization

- **Name and subtitle.** Apple allows 30 characters for each. Example: "Try Something New: Things to Do" and subtitle "Date ideas, hobbies, bucket list". Check that the name is free in both stores before committing.
- **Keywords** (Apple's keyword field, and the Play description): things to do, near me, weekend ideas, date ideas, bucket list, hobbies, new hobby, try new things, activities, city checklist.
- **Screenshots.** The first three do most of the work: the idea card with the one-line promise, the checklist with progress, and a "tried it" card. Use real places in the launch city.
- **Preview video.** 15 to 30 seconds: swipe, pick, go, memory, share card.
- **Ratings.** Ask with the system prompt right after someone rates an activity "Loved it", never on launch or after a "Not for me". Apple limits how often the prompt appears.
- **Korean listing for Seoul,** with Korean screenshots and keywords, when Seoul launches.
- **Update the "What's new" text** with something human every release. It's read more than you'd think.

## 7. Onboarding, retention and the referral loop

**Onboarding.** Show an idea card first. Ask for location only when the person wants nearby places, and offer at-home ideas if they say no. Make the first suggestion an easy one. Explain the app in the cards, not in a tutorial.

**Retention hooks the app already has or plans:** the daily idea widget, weekend ideas on Thursday evening (opt-in), plans with a day and calendar entry, memories, milestones and the monthly recap. Add a reminder on a plan's day. Keep the rule: no streaks and no guilt.

**The referral loop, without tracking people:**

1. Someone does a thing and saves a memory.
2. The app offers the "tried it" card; they post it or send it to the friend who came.
3. The card's link opens a small web page for that activity (and, later, that city) with the store buttons, or the app itself if it's installed.
4. The new person gets the same idea as their first card.

Count this anonymously: the link carries a short code for the *kind* of share (tried-it card, invite, bucket list), not the person, and the Worker counts visits and store taps. That's enough to see which share brings people in.

## 8. Metrics

Set baselines during beta rather than borrowing industry averages, which vary a lot by category.

- **Store conversion:** product page views to installs (App Store Connect and Play Console report this without any code in the app).
- **Activation:** share of new users who plan or mark something done within 7 days. This is the number to improve first.
- **Retention:** people who open the app in week 2 and week 4.
- **The real goal:** things tried per active person per month.
- **Share rate:** shares per memory saved.
- **Loop conversion:** link visits to installs, by share kind.
- **Rating** and the words people use in reviews (steal them for the listing).

In-app numbers need the **opt-in, privacy-friendly analytics** item (TelemetryDeck or Aptabase, anonymous counts). Opt-in will undercount, and that's a fair price for keeping "no tracking" true; it's part of why people would trust the app.

## 11. Budget

The plan works at close to $0 beyond the store accounts (Apple's developer program is $99 a year and Google Play is a one-time $25). Time is the real cost: about an hour a day on videos and community. If there is money, the best uses are, in order: paying local creators for honest posts, printing partner stickers, and small boosted posts behind videos that already did well on their own. Hold off on paid app-install ads until activation and retention look good, or they just buy people who leave.

## 12. Decisions

**Decided 2026-10-31**

- **Budget:** $0 for now. Store accounts are the only cost.
- **Platforms:** TikTok first, because it shows videos to people who don't follow you yet, which is how a new account gets found. Post the same video to Instagram Reels, and use Instagram for Stories (where the share cards go), DMs with local partners and the link in bio. Add YouTube Shorts later with the same videos; it costs nothing extra.
- **Launch city:** Los Angeles, starting with Pasadena. The LA data has about 750 places in and around Pasadena covering 59 of the activities, and drive-time sorting brings in the rest of LA. Pasadena is small enough to cover well and has its own community: r/pasadena, local newsletters, Caltech, PCC and ArtCenter students, and places like Old Pasadena, Eaton Canyon, the Rose Bowl Flea Market and the Huntington. The Rose Parade on January 1 lines up with the New Year launch. Widen to the rest of LA once Pasadena is going.

**Still open**

- **Name.** "Try Something New" is generic, so it's hard to search for and protect. Most short names in this space are already used: several "Sidequest" apps exist (including one for exploring your city), and "Firsts", "First Timers", "Never Done", "Dabble" and "Kindling" are taken on the App Store. Before picking, search both stores and check the handle on TikTok and Instagram.
- **Launch date:** early January for the New Year moment (recommended), or as soon as the store setup is done.
